# Culinary Guide to Barcelona: 18 Catalan Dishes & Must-Try Eateries

**Introduction**  
Barcelona’s culinary landscape is deeply rooted in Catalan tradition, offering a unique blend of flavor, heritage, and creativity. From simple bread dishes to rich stews and delicate desserts, the region boasts a diverse array of dishes that reflect its history and culture. This guide highlights **18 typical meals, snacks, and delicacies specific to Catalonia and Spain**, along with **10 top restaurants and street food spots** where you can experience them, all within a budget of **€20–25 per person**.

---

## Part 1: 18 Typical Catalan Dishes & Delicacies

1. **Pa amb Tomàquet** (Bread with Tomato)  
   The quintessential Catalan starter or main, featuring crusty bread rubbed with ripe tomato sauce, seasoned with salt and olive oil. Simple yet flavorful, it can be ordered as a main or alongside other dishes.

2. **Pa amb Pebre** (Bread with Pepper)  
   Similar to pa amb tomàquet, but with a generous sprinkle of black pepper, adding a spicy kick. A staple at many Catalan eateries.

3. **Pa amb Vi** (Bread with Wine)  
   Thick slices of bread soaked and rubbed with red wine, providing a rich, savory taste. A popular choice for a hearty meal.

4. **Pa amb Sardes** (Bread with Sardines)  
   Whole sardines grilled or baked, mashed with tomato sauce, and spread on crusty bread. A delicious way to enjoy Catalan sardines.

5. **Arroz Negre** (Black Rice)  
   A traditional rice dish cooked with black rice (*carn*) and various meats, often with a rich broth and vegetables. A staple in Catalan family meals and restaurants.

6. **Bomba** (Catalan Potato Dish)  
   Signature Catalan potato dish where potatoes are boiled, mashed, and seasoned with olive oil, garlic, and sometimes pork or chicken. Creamy and uniquely Catalan.

7. **Sobrasada** (Catalan Sausage)  
   Cured meat from Catalonia, typically made from pork, with a firm texture and rich flavor. It can be grilled, pan-fried, or served as part of a meal.

8. **Botifarra** (Catalan Bacon)  
   Another cured meat, similar to sobrasada but with a different curing process, offering a distinct flavor. Often served as a main or side.

9. **Jamón Ibérico de Bellota** (Ibérico Ham with Acorn)  
   A delicacy featuring ham from Iberian pigs, typically from acorn-fed animals. With a subtle, nutty flavor, it's a premium item often ordered as a main.

10. **Patatas Bravas** (Catalan Spicy Potato)  
    Fried potato slices topped with a spicy, garlicky tomato sauce, often with fried eggs or chorizo. The Catalan version is known for its bold, peppery flavor.

11. **Xulle** (Blood Soup)  
    A traditional Catalan soup made with beef blood, served with bread. A unique and hearty dish reflecting the region's culinary heritage, though less common for tourists.

12. **Gambas i Creuades** (Fried Fish and Shrimp)  
    A classic Catalan seafood dish featuring fried snapper or similar fish and shrimp, often with a sauce. Refreshing and highlighting the region's seafood.

13. **Pimientos de Padron** (Catalan Peppers)  
    Small, wrinkled peppers from the Padron family, known for their unique flavor and texture. Used in Catalan cooking as a side dish or ingredient.

14. **Pa amb Tomàquet i Oli** (Bread with Tomato and Oil)  
    A variation of pa amb tomàquet, with a rich tomato sauce enriched with olive oil, adding a more savory note. A favorite among locals.

15. **Crema Catalana** (Catalan Cream Cake)  
    A sweet Catalan dessert made with butter, egg yolks, and sugar, topped with Catalan cream. A classic end to a meal, often served with coffee.

16. **Bolses** (Catalan Stew/Soup)  
    A traditional stew or soup, typically made with meats and vegetables, with a rich broth and a comforting, slow-cooked quality. A staple of Catalan home cooking.

17. **Pa amb Tomàquet i Crema** (Bread with Tomato and Cream)  
    An elaborate version of pa amb tomàquet, with a rich, creamy tomato sauce, adding a luxurious touch to the classic dish.

18. **Cava** (Catalan Sparkling Wine)  
    While not a dish, Cava is an essential accompaniment to Catalan cuisine, known for its crisp, fruity flavors and aging process. It pairs beautifully with the above dishes.
