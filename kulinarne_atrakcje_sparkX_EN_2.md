**Culinary Guide to Barcelona: Authentic Catalan and Spanish Dishes**

**Introduction**
Barcelona’s culinary landscape is a vibrant tapestry of Catalan cuisine, renowned for its bold flavors, fresh ingredients, and time-honored recipes. This guide focuses on the quintessential Catalan and Spanish dishes that truly define the city’s gastronomy, exploring 20 typical meals, snacks, and delicacies. We have curated an extensive list of restaurants, street food spots, and eateries where you can experience these authentic dishes, all within a price range of **€20–25 per person**. The selection avoids generic options like pizza or burgers, instead highlighting items distinct to Catalonia and broader Spanish traditions.

---

### 1. 20 Typical Catalan Meals, Snacks & Delicacies

1. **Pa amb Tomàquet**  
   A classic Catalan snack, consisting of a slice of bread topped with sliced tomatoes, often drizzled with olive oil. A staple breakfast or tapas item, frequently enjoyed with jamón or cheese.

2. **Escalivada**  
   Grilled vegetables (eggplant, zucchini, peppers, etc.) seasoned with salt, olive oil, and sometimes garlic. This tapas highlights local freshness and offers a perfect balance of flavors and textures.

3. **Patatas Brunyoles**  
   Crispy-fried potatoes with a smoky, salty flavor, typically served with cured jamón. The crispy exterior contrasts with the tender interior, making it an irreplaceable tapas staple.

4. **Calçots**  
   Roasted red peppers cooked over an open fire, traditionally served in spring during the Calçotada festival. They symbolize Catalan gastronomy and are enjoyed with sauces like patatas brunyoles or calçat.

5. **Calçat**  
   A hearty soup with roasted vegetables, often including calçots, and sometimes with chicken or beef. It reflects the rustic, comforting nature of Catalan home cooking.

6. **Fideuà**  
   Fried noodles cooked in a creamy, rich sauce with seafood or meat. Serving as both a dinner dish and a tapas, fideuà is a signature of Catalan cuisine, known for its thick, flavorful broth.

7. **Bombas**  
   Catalan meatballs made from pork, seasoned with pimentón (smoked paprika), garlic, and other spices. Served with sauce, they are a classic tapas that delights the palate.

8. **Jamones de la Pedrera**  
   Locally cured pork sausages from the historic Pedrera region of Catalonia. Used in sandwiches, tapas, and as a snack, they are prized for their quality and authentic flavor.

9. **Xulleta de l'Aiguafria**  
   Slow-roasted pork belly from Barcelona’s Aiguafria region, a traditional specialty. It is served with various sauces and is a centerpiece of a hearty Catalan meal.

10. **Sarrasina**  
    A traditional Catalan roast of suckling pig, prepared with a focus on quality and slow cooking. It is a festive dish, often served with multiple sauces and sides.

11. **Crema Catalana**  
    A rich, spiced custard tart made with almonds, cinnamon, and other ingredients. This beloved sweet dessert is closely associated with Catalan patrician cuisine.

12. **Coca de la Morena**  
    A traditional Catalan cake with a caramelized golden crust and a filling of chocolate and spices. A sweet, celebratory treat with a distinctive flavor profile.

13. **Coca de Sant Antoni**  
    Another iconic Catalan cake, known for its unique texture and sweet, aromatic profile. It has a long history and is a popular dessert in Catalan homes.

14. **Turron**  
    Candied fruit confections, a Catalan delicacy made with fresh fruits, sugar, and spices. These sweet delicacies are symbols of Catalan gastronomy, especially enjoyed during festive occasions.

15. **Escudella i Calçot**  
    A traditional Catalan soup containing diced vegetables, chicken, or beef, and calçots (or roasted peppers). It is a comforting, savory soup that embodies regional flavors.

16. **Patata Brava**  
    Savory potato croquettes (often with a spicy, tangy sauce), a popular snack or light meal. They are crispy and seasoned with pimentón and other spices.

17. **Empanada de Carn**  
    A savory empanada (pie) filled with meat, usually pork or chicken, with a thin, crispy crust. It is a traditional Catalan snack, often enjoyed with tapas or as a main dish.

18. **Calçotada**  
    The annual festival where calçots are roasted over fire and enjoyed with various sauces, tapas, and drinks. It is a communal, festive experience that showcases the best of Catalan street food.

19. **Botifarra i Bombas**  
    A combination of sliced pork belly (botifarra) and meatballs (bombas), offering a hearty tapas selection. This pairing highlights the versatility of Catalan cured meats.

20. **Fideuà de Mar**  
    Seafood fideuà, featuring shrimp, clams, mussels, and other seafood, cooked in a creamy broth. A luxurious yet accessible Catalan dish, perfect for seafood enthusiasts.

### 3. Tips for Exploring Catalan Cuisine in Barcelona

- **Timing is key for Calçots:** Calçots are available and delicious only in spring, from March to May. Plan your visit to experience the true Calçotada festival.
- **Embrace street food:** Barcelona’s streets are alive with street food vendors, from calçoterias to fideuà stalls. These offer the most authentic, no-fuss experiences of local cuisine.
- **Visit traditional eateries:** For a more formal dining experience, visit historic restaurants like Gran Mestre or La Mallorca, where traditional recipes and local ingredients are central.
- **Price considerations:** While some Catalan dishes can be pricey, the listed restaurants and spots offer excellent value within the €20–25 per person budget, especially with set menus or tapas selections.
- **Pair with drinks:** Catalan cuisine pairs beautifully with local wines from Catalonia, or with fresh juices and beers from local producers.
